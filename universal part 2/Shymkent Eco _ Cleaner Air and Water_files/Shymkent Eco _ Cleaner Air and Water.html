Cleaner air starts with local action.

Shymkent is one of Kazakhstan’s largest and fastest-growing cities, and its air quality is affected by dense traffic, industrial activity, construction dust, and seasonal weather patterns that trap pollution near the ground.

What drives the pollution?

In Shymkent, air quality is shaped by several everyday city systems working together.

🚗

Traffic emissions

Dense traffic, older vehicles, and road congestion add fine particles and gases to the city air.

🏗️

Construction and dust

Ongoing building projects, roadwork, and open soil disturb particles that linger in the air.

🏭

Industry and heating

Fuel combustion, heating systems, and industrial activity can push pollution levels higher in winter.

The cost of pollution is personal

Pollution is not an abstract environmental issue. It shapes the air people breathe on the way to school, the water families depend on, and how safe it feels to spend time outdoors.

🫁

Health follows you home

Dirty air does not stop at the roadside. Fine particles can reach deep into the lungs, irritate airways, and make existing breathing and heart conditions harder to manage.
👧

Children deserve a safe start

Children breathe more air relative to their size and are still growing. Cleaner routes to school, less traffic exhaust, and safer places to play are investments in their health and future.
🚰

Safe water is non-negotiable

Leaking pipes, overloaded sewers, and inadequate wastewater treatment can put contamination where it does not belong. Prevention protects households and is safer than responding after pollution spreads.
🏙️

A city should be lived in

When air quality is poor, outdoor activity becomes less appealing and families have fewer comfortable ways to use parks, streets, and public spaces. Cleaner neighborhoods improve everyday life for everyone.
Prevention is the stronger choice.

Cleaner transport, controlled construction dust, reliable sewer systems, and treated wastewater address causes at their source. That means fewer people exposed, less strain on public services, and a healthier city for the next generation.

City context

Shymkent is a major southern city, and its growth brings more vehicles, more construction, and greater pressure on the local environment.

Health risk

Repeated exposure to fine particles can worsen respiratory symptoms and reduce quality of life, especially in dense urban neighborhoods.

Water and sewer infrastructure

In fast-growing areas, sewer capacity and aging water pipes may not keep up with demand. Leaks and wastewater overflows can contaminate water sources, so pipe repairs and expanded treatment are important for public health.

Energy shift

Cleaner power, electric mobility, and better urban planning can lower emissions without sacrificing economic growth.

What residents can do

  • Use cleaner transport options when possible, such as carpooling or public transit.
  • Reduce unnecessary idling in traffic and near schools, homes, and bus stops.
  • Support greener urban design, tree planting, and dust control in public spaces.
  • Pay attention to high-pollution days and reduce outdoor exercise during peak exposure.

Paths to cleaner air

Cleaner air and water in Shymkent require action on city planning, solar power, mobility, and essential infrastructure.

Low-carbon power

Prioritize solar farms and rooftop panels over nuclear plants. Shymkent’s hot, sunny climate offers strong potential for solar generation; batteries and grid upgrades can help balance supply after sunset.

Cleaner transport

Electric and hybrid cars reduce tailpipe emissions in traffic-heavy corridors, especially during regular commuting peaks.

Water system upgrades

Repair leaking pipes, expand sewer capacity, and improve wastewater treatment to reduce the risk of dirty water entering local supplies.

1. Solar power

Use solar farms and rooftop panels as an alternative to nuclear plants, pairing them with battery storage and grid improvements for more dependable power.

2. Electric and hybrid cars

Switching to electric and hybrid vehicles can cut exhaust pollution in traffic-heavy neighborhoods and improve urban air quality.

3. Sewer and pipe upgrades

Expand sewer capacity, repair aging water pipes, and improve wastewater treatment to help prevent leaks and sewage from contaminating water.

4. Better transport

Expand public transit, reduce congestion, and improve vehicle maintenance to lower emissions across the city.

5. Green spaces

Plant more trees and maintain parks to absorb particles and cool neighborhoods.

6. Dust control

Use cleaner construction practices, road maintenance, and dust suppression on busy sites.

Current city reality

  • Factories and industrial plants release smoke, dust, and gases.
  • Older vehicles burn fuel and worsen congestion in dense roads.
  • Construction sites spread airborne particles and soil dust.
  • Coal, oil, and fossil-based heating add more pollutants in winter.
  • Without adequate sewers, untreated wastewater can seep through soil and contaminate groundwater.

Main problems

  • Higher emissions in crowded urban districts and traffic corridors.
  • Public health stress on children, older people, and workers.
  • Reduced air quality during cold, stagnant weather periods.
  • More pressure on public spaces, transport, and daily life.

Future cleaner model

  • Solar panels generate low-carbon electricity from Shymkent’s abundant sunshine.
  • Electric cars and hybrid vehicles reduce tailpipe emissions.
  • Cleaner industrial systems lower smoke and harmful combustion output.
  • Drains and sealed sewers carry wastewater to treatment, protecting soil and groundwater.

Expected benefits

  • Lower city pollution near roads, schools, and residential zones.
  • Battery storage and grid upgrades help make solar power available beyond sunny hours.
  • Less wastewater leaking into underground water and nearby waterways.
  • Reduced dependence on high-emission fossil energy sources.
